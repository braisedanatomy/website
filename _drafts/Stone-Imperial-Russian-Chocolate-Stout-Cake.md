<h5>This is an almost direct rip from the ultimate food journal, <a href="http://smittenkitchen.com/2006/11/ganached-guinness-goodness/">Smitten Kitchen</a>.  However, I do credit myself with one thing: walking to Coventry in Cleveland to find a stout that could replace Guinness in the recipe.</h5>
<h5>Guinness is great, but its bitter aftertaste pierces through even the sweetest baked goods.  I needed something just as rich, but unfortunately I know as much about beers as I know about microbiology.  At a friend's suggestion I visited the <a href="http://www.lacaveduvin.com/">Wine Cave</a> for advice.  It's a dark, subdued bar with intimacy-promoting nooks and some very knowledgable bartenders.  When I spoke to one about my recipe, he all but knelt in prayer before the <span style="text-decoration: underline;"><a href="http://www.stonebrew.com/irs/">Stone Imperial Russian Stout</a></span>, the beer I ended up taking home.  I don't have a reason to visit the Cave frequently, but it'll be my first choice for any subsequent boozy cooking adventures.</h5>
<h5>The cake itself isn't overtly sweet, which will set it apart from other deserts.  And its adult flavor is punched up by the coffee ganache.  I ended up baking it in a loaf pan, splitting the loaf in half, and creating a layer cake with ganache in the center and on the top.  I'm not entirely sure of the baking time, but I do know you need to take it out of the oven as soon as it sets.  This cake will dry out quickly after that time.</h5>
