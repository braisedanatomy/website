<h5>The hospital's air conditioning is set permanently to "early fall," and every day around 4pm I find myself dreaming about a fitting dinner.  Hot soup always sounds good.  Maybe some sort of stew.  Or maybe something with a long bake, like a casserole.</h5>
<h5>Then I step outside into the desert of the real: it's August in the midwest and I can't breathe without breaking a sweat.  My dreams of supper quickly turn to freezer pops.</h5>
<h5>This is a cold soup I made with an amended <a href="/2010/09/22/homemade-yogurt/">yogurt</a> recipe -- I've started incubating the yogurt for as many as 18 hours, as per Ari's preference, to get a much tangier batch.  In India, yogurt or seasoned yogurt is used as a cooling dip or condiment in most Indian meals.  And if anyone knows about deep heat, it's those people.</h5>
Ingredients
<h5>This soup is pretty thin if you use plain yogurt, but it was good accompanied with a hearty bread.  If you're looking for something thicker, you might try strained (Greek) yogurt, which is <a href="/2010/12/08/greek-strained-yogurt/">pretty easy to make yourself</a>.</h5>
<ul>
	<li>3 cups of plain yogurt (<a href="/2010/09/22/homemade-yogurt/">mine </a>has become nice and sour this summer)</li>
	<li>1/2 cup chopped cilantro</li>
	<li>1 cucumber, chopped</li>
	<li>1 tsp cumin</li>
	<li>1 tsp chili powder</li>
	<li>salt and pepper to taste</li>
	<li>garlic chips: 5-6 cloves of garlic sliced and fried in a tablespoon of sesame oil</li>
</ul>
<h5>Blend.  Garnish with more chopped cilantro and the garlic chips.  Serve with bread.  Ta da!</h5>
