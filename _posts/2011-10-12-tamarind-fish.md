<h5>Do you ever get Indian food hangovers?  I keep trying to cook with this seed called <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fenugreek">fenugreek</a> (both it and its leaf counterpart are also called <em>methi</em>).  It's a tiny packet of pungence that adds a slight bitter kick to any dish.  The smell can be described as a sort of spicy maple syrup.  It usually gets fished out of the fridge and added to the pan when I'm feeling a little brazen.</h5>
<h5>And it all seems like a good life decision until the next morning, when I wake up smelling like fenugreek incarnate.  I'm my own regrettable one-night stand.  Until after a run and shower to purge it from my system, I'm a little ashamed to go out in public.  That's the only downside to this cuisine: to eat it is to exude it.</h5>
<h5>That said, this fish dish is pretty flexible.  If you don't have fenugreek, or even tamarind paste, you should try it anyway and tell me if it works for you.<span style="color: #444444; font-family: Georgia, 'Times New Roman', 'Bitstream Charter', Times, serif;"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/2011/10/TamarindFish21-1024x682.jpg" alt="" width="640" height="426" /></span></h5>
<div>Ingredients</div>
<div>
	<li><span style="line-height: 19px;">4 white fish filets</span></li>
	<li><span style="line-height: 19px;">Marinade: 2 Tbsp each of dark soy sauce, tomato paste, and plain yogurt</span></li>
	<li><span style="line-height: 19px;">Curry:</span></li>
	<li><span style="line-height: 19px;"> </span><span style="font-size: xx-small;">1 diced onion</span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: xx-small;">a few curry leaves (optional)</span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: xx-small;">2-3 tsp cumin powder</span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: xx-small;">2 tsp coriander powder</span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: xx-small;">2 tsp chili powder</span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: xx-small;">1 Tbsp minced garlic</span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: xx-small;">1 Tbsp minced ginger</span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: xx-small;">1 tsp fenugreek seeds (optional)</span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: xx-small;">1 tsp mustard seeds</span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: xx-small;">1/2 cup plain yogurt</span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: xx-small;">2 Tbsp tomato paste</span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: xx-small;">1 Tbsp tamarind paste (optional...even if it's in the title)</span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: xx-small;">4 Tbsp vegetable oil</span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: xx-small;">salt</span></li>
</div>
<div><span style="font-size: small;"><span style="line-height: 24px;">Marinate the fish with the marinade for at least 30 minutes in the fridge. </span></span></div>
<div><span style="font-size: small;"><span style="line-height: 24px;">Heat the oil in a deep pan over medium high heat, and pan-fry the fish until they are browned on either side and getting crispy (try to only cook each side once).  Transfer the fish to a paper towel-lined plate.  Next add the mustard and fenugreek and allow them to cook in the oil alone for 15 seconds (you may have to cover the pan if they start to pop).  Add the cumin, coriander, and chili powder, the garlic and ginger, and mix for about 10 additional seconds.  Add the yogurt, tomato paste, and the tamarind paste, and then gently add the fish back to the pan.  Allow to simmer for 10 additional minutes, adding water as necessary if the mixture starts to look dry.  Adjust with salt to taste.</span></span></div>
