<div><span style="font-size: small;">The first post installment is the Green </span><a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galette"><span style="font-size: small;">Galette</span></a><span style="font-size: small;">, a variation of the </span><a href="http://smittenkitchen.com/2008/10/cabbage-and-mushroom-galette/"><span style="font-size: small;">Smitten Kitchen</span></a><span style="font-size: small;"> edition.  The galette is a rustic pie that should yield a comforting filling and a satisfyingly stable crust without the fussiness of a pie pan or a lattice top.</span>
<span style="font-size: small;">It's "Green" because it makes the dish sound invitingly fresh and invigorating, so readers aren't scared away by the 6 cups of cabbage it bears. </span>
<span style="font-size: small;">
</span></div>
<div><span style="font-size: small;">Don't be afraid of cabbage. </span></div>
<div>
<ul>
	<li><span style="font-size: small;">If you treat it nicely and don't leave it on the heat for too long, cooked cabbage doesn't have to look like a B-movie sea creature.</span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: small;">Raw cabbage keeps for quite a while in the fridge, making it as likely a staple as ramen.</span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: small;"><a href="http://www.whfoods.com/genpage.php?tname=foodspice&amp;dbid=19">It's good for you</a>.  And food is the only thing we can prescribe until we graduate.</span></li>
</ul>
<div><span style="font-size: small;">
</span></div>
<div><strong><span style="font-size: small;">Ingredients</span></strong></div>
</div>
<div><span style="font-size: small;">1 frozen pie dough</span></div>
<div><span style="font-size: small;">2 tbsp butter</span></div>
<div><span style="font-size: small;">1 large yellow onion, chopped into nail-sized pieces</span></div>
<div><span style="font-size: small;">1-2 tablespoons fresh dill (this was city fresh windfall, you can use dried)</span></div>
<div><span style="font-size: small;">1/2 zucchini, chopped as per the onion</span></div>
<div><span style="font-size: small;">6 cups (1/2 head) green cabbage cut into thin strips </span></div>
<div><span style="font-size: small;">1/4 cup cottage cheese</span></div>
<div><span style="font-size: small;">1 hard boiled egg, chopped</span></div>
<div><span style="font-size: small;">2 tbsp cider vinegar</span></div>
<div><span style="font-size: small;">salt</span></div>
<div><span style="font-size: small;">pepper from a grinder</span></div>
<div><span style="font-size: small;">
</span>
<span style="font-size: small;">Pull the pie dough out of the freezer to give it enough time to thaw.  Melt the butter in a large skillet, then add the onion, dill, and zucchini and saute until softened (10 minutes).  Add the cabbage and 1/2 tsp of salt, and turn all of the vegetables together with tongs or a spatula.  After 2 minutes, add 1/4 cup of water and cover so that the cabbage can wilt down a bit.  I'm not going to tell you a time for this -- just keep testing the cabbage so that it remains green but you can pick up a piece you can bite into it easily.  Don't cook it for too long or it will turn grey.  Add more water if necessary, but when you are done the mixture shouldn't be very wet.  Turn off the heat and add the cottage cheese, egg, vinegar, and salt/pepper to taste. </span>
<span style="font-size: small;">Preheat the oven to 400F, unroll the pie dough, and put the filling in the center leaving 2-3 inches on all sides. Then use the remaining dough on the perimeter to make pleats that will hold in the filling.  You should get 6-7 pleats before you're done.  Bake the galette on a flat pan (parchment is a nice touch) for about 20 minutes.  Cool on a wire rack, and cut into wedges.</span></div>
