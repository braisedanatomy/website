<h5>Not every recipe you try is gonna be a success. Not every successful recipe will be something you want to try again...</h5>
<h5>A couple of months ago my mom bequeathed to me one of her many fantastic thrift store finds - <a href="http://www.amazon.com/Norpro-3114-Danish-Aebleskiver-Pan/dp/B000F741O4">a cast iron skillet that looked like it had gotten into a fight with a mellon baller</a>. With a little help from my dear friend google, I was able to figure out it was an aebleskiver pan. And what is an <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%86bleskiver">aebleskiver</a>?? It's essentially a fried pancake ball. Well, with a definition like that, how could I resist a little culinary experiment?</h5>
<h5>The results: Are they delicious? Of course (fried balls of apples and dough?! how can you go wrong?). How about a pain in the you-know-what? More than I care to admit. Will I be making these again? Not any time soon. To be honest there was just too much oil in the recipe (even after I cut it in thirds) and it was way too much work for the final product. I will say that the batter itself is quite tasty and makes some delicious regular ol' flapjacks when cooked in a frying pan.</h5>
<h5>Oh well. At least I can now say that I know how to make aebleskivers...</h5>
<a href="/wp-content/uploads/2012/03/series2.jpg"><img class="aligncenter size-large wp-image-3729" title="too much work..." src="/wp-content/uploads/2012/03/series2-1010x1024.jpg" alt="" width="640" height="648" /></a><em>(Adapted from Allrecipes's <a href="http://allrecipes.com/recipe/aebleskiver/">Aebleskiver Recipe</a>)</em>

What you need (makes about 30 aebleskivers):
<ul>
	<li>2 egg whites</li>
	<li>2 cups AP flour</li>
	<li>2 tsp baking powder</li>
	<li>1 tbs sugar</li>
	<li>1/2 tsp baking soda</li>
	<li>1/2 tsp salt</li>
	<li>2 egg yolks</li>
	<li>4 tbs butter, melted</li>
	<li>2 cups buttermilk (Or <a href="http://www.ehow.com/how_4868843_make-buttermilk-milk.html">two cups regular milk plus 2 tbs distilled vinegar. Mix then let sit for 5 minutes</a>)</li>
	<li>2 apples, cut into small cubes</li>
	<li>1 tsp vegetable oil per aebleskiver (ack!)</li>
</ul>
<div><span style="font-size: small;"><span style="line-height: 24px;">What you need to do:</span></span></div>
<div>
<ol>
	<li><span style="font-size: 12px; line-height: 18px;">Using an electric mixer, beat the egg whites in a glass bowl until they can hold stiff peaks (see above).</span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: 12px; line-height: 18px;">In a separate bowl, mix together the flour, baking soda, salt, baking powder, sugar, egg yolks, butter and buttermilk. Beat until smooth. </span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: 12px; line-height: 18px;">Gently fold the egg whites into the batter.</span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: 12px; line-height: 18px;">Spoon about 1 tsp of vegetable oil in the bottom of each cup in the aebleskiver pan and heat.</span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: 12px; line-height: 18px;">Pour about 1 tbs of the batter into each cup, add a pinch of apples to each cup then cover with another tbs of batter.</span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: 12px; line-height: 18px;">When the batter starts to bubble at the edges, use some sort of cooking utensil to flip them over (I used a fork and spoon, but traditionally you use two long knitting needles...I am anything but traditional).</span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: 12px; line-height: 18px;">Continue cooking until both sides are a light brown.</span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: 12px; line-height: 18px;">Tops with powdered sugar, whipped cream or maple syrup (or all three) and enjoy!</span></li>
</ol>
</div>
