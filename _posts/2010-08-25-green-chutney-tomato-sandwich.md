<h5>Check out what my mom used to punish me when I misbehaved as a child:</h5>
<h5><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2010/08/Coconut-Grater.jpg"><img class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-358" title="Coconut Grater" src="/wp-content/uploads/2010/08/Coconut-Grater.jpg" alt="(c) http://ncmoa.org" width="300" height="229" /></a>Just kidding!  That thing is actually a grater, and she and my grandmother use a tool that looks very similar to scrape out the inner flesh of coconuts.  One of my favorite things they make with it is a green chutney that stars in a vegetarian sandwich.   Yesterday I remembered some coconut in the fridge that I've had longer than I'd like to admit, and I decided to make it.  While, in Cleveland, coconut is a well-traveled food, the other main ingredients came from <a href="http://cityfresh.org/">City Fresh</a> and our intrepid garden.</h5>
<a href="/wp-content/uploads/2010/08/Gchutney1.jpg"><img class="aligncenter size-large wp-image-361" title="Gchutney1" src="/wp-content/uploads/2010/08/Gchutney1-1024x682.jpg" alt="I actually fell off my bike and crushed that jalapeno inside my grocery bag. That's why I had to make this." width="640" height="426" /></a>
<h5>Think of this as an updated watercress sandwich.  The taste is really fresh, and the (coconut) haters may not even notice that there's coconut up in there.</h5>
<h5>Ingredients</h5>
<ul>
	<li><span style="font-size: small;"><span style="line-height: 19px;">1/3-1/2 cup grated unsweetened coconut (I used dry, you can use frozen also)</span></span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: small;"><span style="line-height: 19px;">1 cup packed cilantro, washed</span></span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: small;"><span style="line-height: 19px;">2 cloves garlic</span></span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: small;"><span style="line-height: 19px;">1 jalapeño pepper, stem/ribs/seeds removed</span></span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: small;"><span style="line-height: 19px;">juice from 1/2 lemon</span></span></li>
	<li><span style="line-height: 19px; font-size: small;">1/2-1 tsp salt</span></li>
</ul>
<h5><span style="line-height: 15px;">Place all of the ingredients in the food processor and blend until you've got a pesto consistency.  You may need to scrape down the sides and add water periodically.  I ended up using maybe 1/4 cup.  Serve in a sandwich with slices of tomato and mild cheese.</span></h5>
