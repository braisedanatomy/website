<h5>When I started medical school, no one ever told me how hard anatomy would be.  Sure, I was apprised of the <strong>volume</strong> of subject material (truly: in 2011 I've forgotten more about the intrinsic muscles of the foot than I will ever know about anything else).  What I wasn't aware of was how <em>bad</em> I would be at dissection.  The textbooks detailed the aesthetics of the final product, but the directions were regrettably abstract.  Only the perfectionists and artists in our class seemed to fare well.  Ari and I even got to do it <em>twice</em> because of the masters of anatomy; unfortunately, I never got past the chop suey skill level.</h5>
<h5>Over winter break I decided to construct a coleslaw to pair with the Smitten Kitchen <a href="http://smittenkitchen.com/2010/01/southwestern-pulled-brisket/">Southwestern Pulled Brisket</a>.  Wasn't sure how to go about shredding the cabbage, but I was too proud to ask for help.  I began hacking away at the vegetable much in the way I executed most of anatomy lab -- at the wrong angle with a dull knife.</h5>
<p style="text-align: center;"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2011/02/SesameColeslaw2.jpg"><img class="aligncenter size-large wp-image-2041" title="Bowls courtesy of my sister and brother-in-law from Crate &amp; Barrel.  C&amp;B, if you're reading this...how about a sponsorship??" src="/wp-content/uploads/2011/02/SesameColeslaw2-1024x802.jpg" alt="" width="640" height="501" /></a></p>

<h5>My family watched in horrified silence for a couple of minutes before my mom offered to show me the right way to cole a slaw.  It turns out that I was trying to work from the outside of the globe in, when in fact you need to start from the core out.  Although I was resentful of the advice at the time, this lesson has aided every subsequent cabbage encounter.</h5>
<h5>I'm sure a lot of you already have this basic skill, but I've prepared a mini tutorial (see below) to facilitate your cabbage dissection experience, should you not.  Ari, would you be interested in co-producing a <a href="http://www.amazon.com/Atlas-Human-Anatomy-Student-Consult/dp/1416059512">Netter's</a> of chopped vegetables?</h5>
<h5>Ingredients (adapted from the Simply Recipes <a href="http://simplyrecipes.com/recipes/asian_coleslaw/">Asian Coleslaw</a>):</h5>
<ul>
	<li>3/4 shredded cabbage</li>
	<li>3-4 carrots chopped into matchsticks</li>
	<li>2 Tbsp tahini (or peanut butter)</li>
	<li>2 Tbsp sesame oil</li>
	<li>4 Tbsp rice wine vinegar</li>
	<li>1 Tbsp brown sugar</li>
	<li>2-3 tsp powdered ginger</li>
	<li>1 tsp salt</li>
	<li>Ground black pepper, sesame seeds, and some chopped cilantro</li>
	<li>Bean sprouts <em>(optional addition suggested by Ari, the whitest Asian I know)</em></li>
</ul>
