<h5><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2012/05/IMG_6428.jpg"><img class="aligncenter size-large wp-image-3901" title="IMG_6428" src="/wp-content/uploads/2012/05/IMG_6428-1024x682.jpg" alt="" width="640" height="426" /></a>If I've known you for longer than a week and we've become friends, chances are I've asked you about dim sum.  "Do you like dim sum?"  "Where do you go?"  "What's your favorite thing to get?"</h5>
<h5>Somehow, sitting at a table with carts of food circling us like sharks is the ultimate bond of friendship.  Despite obsessing over it, I only end up eating dim sum once a year.  And the annual glut is followed by the annual guilt of eating far more than my fair share of whatever we chose.</h5>
<h5>My favorite things are #1 <a href="http://farm2.static.flickr.com/1196/1247051047_d7ef4e497b.jpg">BBQ pork buns</a> that are as fluffy as clouds, and #2 the <a href="http://www.unglamorouslove.com/wp-content/uploads/2010/02/sesame-ball.jpg">fried sesame balls</a> with sweet read bean paste inside.  I bought a can of this bean paste at the Asian store and decided to put it in some pop tarts.</h5>
<h5>There's no recipe because I followed the instructions verbatim from <a href="http://smittenkitchen.com/2010/04/homemade-pop-tarts/">Smitten Kitchen</a> and she did a better job of delineating the steps than I ever could -- let me know if you try it.</h5>
