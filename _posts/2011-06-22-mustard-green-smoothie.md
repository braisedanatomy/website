<h5>A friend of mine has <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulcerative_colitis">ulcerative colitis</a>, an autoimmune disease of the lower intestine.  A big part of his health routine (and what should be for all of us meat-and-taters loving Americans) is to add a lot of fiber to his diet.  One of the ways he does this is by drinking a smoothie in the evening with added greens.  If you study health or are interested in it, you know that fiber is the roto-rooter of the intestines.  Fiber, especially for those with irritable bowel disease, is one of the best ways to prevent colon cancer.</h5>
<h5><a href="http://www.whfoods.com/genpage.php?tname=nutrient&amp;dbid=59">Here</a> is a list of high-fiber foods.</h5>
<a href="/wp-content/uploads/2011/06/GreenSmoothie2.jpg"><img class="aligncenter size-large wp-image-2625" title="GreenSmoothie2" src="/wp-content/uploads/2011/06/GreenSmoothie2-1024x682.jpg" alt="" width="640" height="426" /></a>
<h5>Try the green smoothie, either for breakfast or a snack, for a few days in a row.  It's super simple -- banana, orange, apple, some green leafy vegetable, and a little milk or soymilk.  Add a little sugar if you like, especially if you get adventurous with mustard greens.  The mustard greens themselves are interesting on the first sip, peppery by the 5th, and either electrifying or jarring by the last.</h5>
<h5>Hopefully some of you will try it.  Let's make a movement!  Literally.</h5>
Ingredients
<ul>
	<li>1 banana, sliced</li>
	<li>1 orange, peeled &amp; sliced</li>
	<li>1 apple, sliced</li>
	<li>2 leaves mustard greens, kale, or spinach</li>
	<li>milk or soymilk as needed</li>
	<li>sugar, brown sugar, or honey as needed</li>
</ul>
</div>
<h5>Add the liquid to the blender first, blend at the lowest speed and then build up speed over time.  If it isn't blending well, add more liquid.  Keep tasting to adjust the sweetness you desire.  Feel free to mix and match with different fruits.  Some people also add minced ginger or cooked carrots.</h5>
