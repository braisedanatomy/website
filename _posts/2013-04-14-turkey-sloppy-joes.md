<h5><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2013/04/Closed-Sandwich.jpg"><img class="aligncenter size-large wp-image-4519" title="" src="/wp-content/uploads/2013/04/Closed-Sandwich-1024x682.jpg" alt="" width="640" height="426" /></a><span style="color: #000000; font-size: 1em; line-height: 1.5em;">Remember those old Hasbro commercials about </span><a style="font-size: 1em;" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AQZpDiJmIYk">family game night</a><span style="color: #000000; font-size: 1em; line-height: 1.5em;">? Everyone is getting along. There's no yelling, crying or screaming. The competition is only of the "friendly" persuasion. Yeah...that's not how it works in my family. It may start out that way, but before long I'm sulking in the corner because I'm in last place (I'm a horrible loser), my brother is gloating about his win, my parents are shaking their heads in disbelief at their overgrown babies, and my sister is just trying to get everyone to come back to the table so we can resume the game. There are a few ways to remedy this issue. 1 - Try out a new game no one has ever played before (only works for 1 round before someone "masters it"). 2 - Make loud noises to distract family members while I steal the cards/money/desired pieces (only really improves the situation for me rather than the rest of the family). 3 - Make a lot of food so everyone is busy munching and no one has time to cry/scream/gloat/sulk/etc. One game that is particularly sulk-inducing is poker. Somehow my brother, sister, mother and father all have some talent when it comes to this tricky little game...a talent that I seem to lack. Maybe next time I can distract them</span><span style="color: #000000; font-size: 1em; line-height: 1.5em;"> my new favorite, not-so-tidy, no-utensil-requiring finger food.</span></h5>
<h5>Quick shout-out to my sorority sister, <a href="http://www.pandathebaker.com/">Amanda</a> who provided me with this recipe after my first attempt at a <a href="/2012/04/29/sloppy-fauxs-vegetarian-sloppy-joes/">healthy (read: vegetarian) sloppy joe</a>.</h5>
<a href="/wp-content/uploads/2013/04/series.jpg"><img class="aligncenter size-large wp-image-4520" title="" src="/wp-content/uploads/2013/04/series-1024x335.jpg" alt="" width="640" height="209" /></a>

Ingredients (makes enough for 8 sandwiches):
<div style="font-size: 16px;">
<ul>
	<li><span style="font-size: 16px;">Sloppy Joe Spice: Combine equal parts of the following seasonings - onion powder, garlic powder, coriander, salt, pepper and crushed red pepper flakes (I made a lot and saved it for next time)</span></li>
</ul>
<ul>
	<li><span style="font-size: 16px;">2 sweet onions, diced</span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: 16px;">2 bell peppers, diced</span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: 16px;">2 lbs ground turkey</span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: 16px;">2 tbs sloppy joe spice</span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: 16px;">2 1/2 cups tomato sauce (I usually make <a href="/2012/04/15/matzah-pizza/">this recipe</a>)</span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: 16px;">1/4 cup brown sugar</span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: 16px;">2 tbs worcestershire sauce</span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: 16px;">8 buns (<a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onion_roll">onion buns</a> are the best)</span></li>
</ul>
</div>
<div style="font-size: 16px;">
<ol>
	<li><span style="font-size: 16px;">Sauté the onions and bell peppers in olive oil till soft (5-8 min). </span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: 16px;">Add the two tbs of sloppy joe spice, brown sugar, worcestershire sauce, and tomato sauce to the sautéed veggies, and mix. </span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: 16px;">In a separate pan, brown the turkey.</span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: 16px;">Combine the two pans and simmer for 5-7 minutes (or longer if you want it less saucy).</span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: 16px;">Serve on a bun and enjoy!</span></li>
</ol>
