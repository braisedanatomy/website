<h5>I have a relationship with my crock pot. We've been involved for about a year.</h5>
<h5>It all started last January, when some friends and I went to <a href="http://www.urbanspoon.com/r/16/204586/restaurant/Cleveland/Parma/Udupi-Cafe-Parma-Heights">Udupi Cafe</a> and then to the nearby thrift store. I shuffled around without intending to buy anything until I happened  by the back rack.  The attraction between the crockpot and me was so instantaneous that I sidled up to grab it, checked the price, and walked to the cash register without looking back.</h5>
<h5>On the drive back home, gazing adoringly into my arms, I cooed: "...I love you."</h5>
<h5>"Awww, you do!?" my friend sitting next to me asked hopefully.</h5>
<h5>Awkwardness ensued.</h5>
<a href="/wp-content/uploads/2011/02/BrusselSprouts21.jpg"><img title="The chili garlic sauce is an option.  A delicious option." src="/wp-content/uploads/2011/02/BrusselSprouts21-1024x665.jpg" alt="" width="640" height="415" /></a>
<h5>I've only used it a <a href="http://smittenkitchen.com/2010/01/southwestern-pulled-brisket/">few</a> <a href="http://smittenkitchen.com/2010/01/black-bean-soup-toasted-cumin-seed-crema/">times</a> <a href="http://www.foodnetwork.com/recipes/alton-brown/overnight-oatmeal-recipe/index.html">since</a> <a href="http://crockpot365.blogspot.com/2008/04/crockpot-16-bean-soup-recipe.html">then</a>, but the crock has never done me wrong.  The recipes that take 8 hours I can let sit quietly overnight.  For the recipes that take up to 3 hours, I can take the pot up to my room and tend to it during homework breaks.  There's nothing more intimate than a slow stir by the light of my monitor.</h5>
<a href="/wp-content/uploads/2011/02/BrusselSprouts4.jpg"><img class="aligncenter size-large wp-image-1924" title="As you can see, there's no actual schoolwork going on here" src="/wp-content/uploads/2011/02/BrusselSprouts4-1024x614.jpg" alt="" width="640" height="383" /></a><span style="color: #000000; font-size: 13px; line-height: 19px;">As it turns out, mustard and brussels sprouts are from the same genus (along with cabbage, turnips, cauliflower, and broccoli) and are sure to soften the resolve of even the most savage Brassica basher.  I think you could make this on the stove top...but believe me when I say that right now there is probably someone chucking a crock pot at a thrift store near you.  Seize the chance for the love of a lifetime.</span>

<span style="font-size: 13px; line-height: 19px;"><a style="color: #000000;" href="/wp-content/uploads/2011/02/BrusselSprouts3.jpg"><img class="aligncenter size-large wp-image-1923" title="Everything dumped in before cooking" src="/wp-content/uploads/2011/02/BrusselSprouts3-1024x760.jpg" alt="" width="640" height="475" /></a>Ingredients (her <a href="http://www.365daysofcrockpot.com/">blog</a> is no longer easily searchable, but this was inspired by the author of this <a href="http://www.365daysofcrockpot.com/2010/01/order-your-cookbook-now.html">book</a>).</span>
<ul>
	<li><span style="font-size: x-small;"><span style="line-height: 19px;">1.5 - 2 Tbsp unsalted butter</span></span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: x-small;"><span style="line-height: 19px;">1 bag frozen brussel sprouts (if you're using fresh ones, I think that amounts to about 40-50.  Use little ones if you can because they cook more quickly).</span></span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: x-small;"><span style="line-height: 19px;">1/4 cup Dijon mustard</span></span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: x-small;"><span style="line-height: 19px;">1-2 Tbsp <a href="http://t3.gstatic.com/images?q=tbn:ANd9GcSC0gVf_KqkgAJNe5DPcPY87r_qXUuzAsjjjO_YzHMs4CqbtzDv">garlic chili sauce</a> (optional)</span></span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: x-small;"><span style="line-height: 19px;">salt and pepper to taste</span></span></li>
</ul>
<h5><span style="font-size: x-small;">Here's the easy part: dump all of these things in, turn your crock pot to high, and stir every 10-15 minutes for 90 minutes or until the brussels sprouts take on some darkness.  Try one--it should have the body of a sprout but the soul of butter and mustard.  Season as desired.  This is intended as a side.  But as always, I have self-control issues.</span></h5>
