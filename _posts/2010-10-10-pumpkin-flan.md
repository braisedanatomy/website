<h5><span style="color: #444444;">Now I never claimed to be good at baking...in fact, the oven terrifies me. The thought of putting a bunch of ingredients in the strange rectangular box, leaving it alone for an hour, and then magically something edible appears is not a concept I am quick to get on board with. I much prefer watching my food cook on the stove top. I can taste it as I go and add a little extra of this, or sprinkle on a little extra of that. I realize, however, that with the responsibility of co-owning a food blog, I need to learn to step outside my comfort box (or comfort stove, dare I say) and take a stab at something new. So in the interest of trying new things I attempted to make not just "flan", but pumpkin flan.</span></h5>
<h5><span style="color: #444444;">I realize that flan tends to have a somewhat negative connotation...and not unreasonably so. Fact is it's a strange gelatinous substance that often tastes strongly of eggs. It's not quite a pudding and it's definitely not a cake. It's...flan. If done correctly, I think it can be quite tasty, but there are lots of opportunities to mess up the recipe...In fact, I experienced quite a few of them while trying to create this dish. Despite all of these opportunities for failure, I think this recipe will satisfy most "flan haters", and with the addition of pumpkin, is rather fitting for the month of halloween. If you're still looking for a reason to make this dish,there is also the promise a </span><a href="http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PgkO6WbBDj8"><span style="color: #444444;">flan-high</span></a><span style="color: #444444;">.</span></h5>
<p style="text-align: center;"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2010/10/IMG_7968.jpg"><img class="aligncenter size-large wp-image-871" title="This photo was actually taken on the street...wonder what we're gonna do when it starts to get cold..." src="/wp-content/uploads/2010/10/IMG_7968-1024x682.jpg" alt="" width="640" height="426" /></a></p>
What you need:
<ul>
	<li>1 and 3/4 cup of sugar</li>
	<li>3 whole eggs</li>
	<li>3 egg yolks</li>
	<li>3 cups of half and half (or 3 cups of cream)</li>
	<li>1/2 cup of pumpkin puree</li>
	<li>1/2 tsp of cinnamon</li>
	<li>1/2 tsp of nutmeg</li>
	<li>pinch of salt</li>
	<li>a 9X9 baking dish and a larger baking dish that the first one can sit inside of</li>
</ul>
<a href="/wp-content/uploads/2010/10/IMG_7947.jpg"><img title="This is what the dish looks like before you flip it." src="/wp-content/uploads/2010/10/IMG_7947-1024x682.jpg" alt="" width="640" height="426" /></a>

What you need to do:
<ol>
	<li>Heat 1 cup of sugar and 1/3 cup of water in a sauce pan on the stove (yay! stove action!). Allow the sugar to caramelize to a brownish color. I am told this should take about 10 minutes (side note: my sugar never did reach a brownish color...not sure why).</li>
	<li>Pour the caramelized sugar in the bottom of the 9X9 in baking dish (do this quickly as it doesn't take long for the sugar to harden).</li>
	<li>Preheat the oven to 350 degrees F.</li>
	<li>Add the remaining sugar (3/4 cup), eggs, egg yolks, and pinch of salt to a bowl and whisk with an electric beater until the mixture is homogenous and pretty thick. Fold in the pumpkin puree until everything is evenly mixed.</li>
	<li>In a sauce pan, heat the half and half, nutmeg and cinnamon for about 3-4 minutes (until the mixture just barely starts to steam).</li>
	<li>Add the milk/cream/spices mixture to the eggs/sugar/pumpkin little by little. Be sure to continuously whisk the mixture as you combine the two.</li>
	<li>Add the contents of the bowl to the 9X9 in baking dish.</li>
	<li>Place the baking dish in the larger baking dish and fill the larger baking dish with boiling water until it is about 1 in. from the top of the smaller baking dish (for an example of what this looks like, see <a href="http://www.google.com/imgres?imgurl=http://girlfriday.typepad.com/photos/coconut_flan/flan_015.jpg&amp;imgrefurl=http://girlfriday.typepad.com/my_weblog/2008/03/recipe-coconut.html&amp;usg=__oqKYttJt2ynH277vmjxQ5zHoRyc=&amp;h=375&amp;w=500&amp;sz=29&amp;hl=en&amp;start=0&amp;zoom=1&amp;tbnid=LdhZIM0wgrc6fM:&amp;tbnh=128&amp;tbnw=197&amp;prev=/images%3Fq%3Dflan%2Bbaking%2Bdish%26um%3D1%26hl%3Den%26biw%3D1116%26bih%3D636%26tbs%3Disch:1&amp;um=1&amp;itbs=1&amp;iact=rc&amp;dur=339&amp;ei=-c6xTIaXIsH_lgf3vq3lDw&amp;oei=686xTPWcJ4OBlAfH_bSrAg&amp;esq=7&amp;page=1&amp;ndsp=15&amp;ved=1t:429,r:3,s:0&amp;tx=125&amp;ty=100">this image</a>.</li>
	<li>Place the 2 baking dishes in the oven and bake for about 1 hour and 15 minutes. It's important to note that the higher the cream content in your recipe, the faster the dish will set. In the future, I plan to use all cream instead of half and half so I don't have to cook it as long.</li>
	<li>After an hour and 15 minutes, remove the dishes and let cool for about 30 minutes.</li>
	<li>After it has cooled, flip the contents onto a serving dish. The caramelized sugar will serve as a sugar glaze. Supposedly if you do this right, there should be a hard sugar layer one top...but I didn't manage to pull that off.</li>
	<li>Enjoy!</li>
</ol>
