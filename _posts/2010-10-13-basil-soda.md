<h5>I find reasons to be grateful for my pre-med education all the time, but rarely inside the medical school.</h5>
<h5><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2010/10/BasilSoda5.jpg"><img class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-830" title="And the taste, you ask?  A friend of ours said &quot;evanescent.&quot;  My reaction was similar to that of the Gap scent &quot;grass&quot;: unexpected, delightful, but I wouldn't stock up on it for the nuclear holocaust." src="/wp-content/uploads/2010/10/BasilSoda5.jpg" alt="" width="899" height="530" /></a><span style="font-size: 13px; color: #333333; line-height: 19px;">I'd never made flavored simple syrup before, and if you had watched me do it that would have been obvious: I threw in 1/2 cup sugar, 1/2 cup water, and 1 cup of our precious garden basil into a shallow pan and boiled until the herb had completely given up the ghost.  And the taste?  Plain sugar syrup.  What went wrong?</span></h5>
<h5><span style="color: #333333;">Then, I remembered:  ugh...organic chemistry lab.  That hellish 2 hour period that stood between me and lunch every Tuesday and Thursday of sophomore year.  In the lab session on distillation, we had to isolate aromatic oils by slowly heating a solution so that the molecules of interest would reach their boiling point, vaporize, and then condense in another place without the other crap in the solution.</span></h5>
<span style="color: #333333;"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2010/10/DistillationGraphic.jpg"><img class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-801" title="I have no opinion about the condenser." src="/wp-content/uploads/2010/10/DistillationGraphic.jpg" alt="" width="360" height="387" /></a></span><span style="font-size: 13px; color: #000000; line-height: 19px;"> </span>
<h5><span style="color: #444444;"><span style="color: #333333;">I suppose in my first attempt I was boiling off all of the flavor and leaving behind the dregs of the plant.  I looked up the </span></span><a href="http://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/volatile%20oil"><span style="color: #444444;"><span style="color: #333333;">volatile oils</span></span></a><span style="color: #444444;"><span style="color: #333333;"> usually found in basil, and the only one that seems to have a boiling point below that of water is <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thymol"><span style="color: #444444;">thymol</span></a> (I welcome anyone, including Ari, to out-nerd me on this one).  But whatever, the addition of basil at a cooler temperature did produce a green, basil-flavored syrup that I added to soda water.</span></span></h5>
<h5><span style="color: #444444;"><span style="color: #333333;">A more popular application of simple syrup is the cocktail.  But I have class tomorrow.</span></span></h5>
<span style="color: #444444;"><span style="color: #333333;"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2010/10/BasilSoda2.jpg"><img class="aligncenter size-large wp-image-833" title="As I was writing this post I read about a method of blanching the leaves and blending them to get a bright green syrup with an even stronger flavor.  There will be updates." src="/wp-content/uploads/2010/10/BasilSoda2-1024x877.jpg" alt="" width="640" height="548" /></a>Ingredients</span></span>
<ul>
	<li>1/2 cup water</li>
	<li>1/2 cup sugar</li>
	<li>1 cup packed basil leaves (cheap at Park2Shop or Zagara's.  I'm trying lavender next)</li>
	<li>12 ounces carbonated water</li>
</ul>
<h5>Boil the water and sugar in a shallow pan over the stove until the sugar is dissolved and the syrup has reduced a bit.  Turn off the heat, and when the mixture is still hot add the basil.  Let steep for 30 minutes, then strain the syrup through a fine mesh strainer into an air-tight container.  Seal the container, and keep (in the fridge? in a dark cool place? for up to a month?  I have no idea) until you're ready to use it.  I added about 3 tablespoons of the syrup to the carbonated water to make the soda.</h5>
