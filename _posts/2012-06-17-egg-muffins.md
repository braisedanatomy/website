<h5></h5>
<h5><span style="color: #000000;"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2012/06/cooked1.jpg"><img class="aligncenter size-large wp-image-3977" title="there are actually only 9 muffins in this tin, but I arranged them to look like it was full...muahahahaha." src="/wp-content/uploads/2012/06/cooked1-1024x682.jpg" alt="" width="640" height="426" /></a>As much as I love food, and am hungry most hours of the day, 4-5am is not one of those times. While I'm perfectly happy waking up that early in the morning, I have to force myself to choke down something resembling breakfast in order to prevent possible fainting episodes during rounds/in the OR. I'm not sure why, but it seems to take my stomach a little while to wake up every morning, which makes it all the more important that I have something I can throw in my pocket to eat once my gag reflex has quieted down enough to swallow my breakfast. One of my favorite make-ahead breakfasts is <a href="/2011/10/30/breakfast-in-a-jar/">oatmeal in a jar</a>, but being the egg lover that I am, I've been looking for a portable dish featuring my favorite ingredient. I had settled on hard boiled eggs (not the most creative, but they did the job) until I came across <a href="http://www.kalynskitchen.com/2006/10/egg-muffins-revisited-again.html">this idea</a>. Within minutes (okay, hours) I was in my kitchen working on my own version of egg muffins. Sure enough, they were delicious, </span><span style="color: #000000;">and more importantly, could be refrigerated to eat at a later date!</span></h5>
<h5>If you choose to take a stab at this recipe, the one major suggestion I'll make is to use silicon muffin cups. When I first made them, I chose to use paper cups (as evidenced by the photos), which <em>technically</em> worked, but it was a pain trying to scrape that last bit of muffin off the paper. I've since purchased <a href="http://www.amazon.com/Chicago-Metallic-Baking-Essentials-Silicone/dp/B0047T6UQI/ref=sr_1_6?ie=UTF8&amp;qid=1339974474&amp;sr=8-6&amp;keywords=silicon+muffin+cups">my own set of silicon cups</a> and I couldn't be happier with the purchase.</h5>

<h5><em><span style="color: #ff0000;">(Adapted from <span style="color: #ff0000;"><a href="http://www.kalynskitchen.com/2006/10/egg-muffins-revisited-again.html">Kalyn's Kitchen</a>)</span></span></em></h5>
Ingredients (makes 8-10 muffins):
<ul>
	<li>6 eggs</li>
	<li>1/2 cup cheddar cheese</li>
	<li>1/4 cup parmesan cheese</li>
	<li>1/4 tsp salt</li>
	<li>1/4 tsp garlic powder</li>
	<li>pepper, to taste</li>
	<li>Veggies of your choosing (I added about 1/3 cup mushrooms and 2-3 tbs chopped green onions)</li>
</ul>
<div>
<ol>
	<li><span style="font-size: small;">Preheat the oven to 375 degrees F.</span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: small;">In a large bowel, whisk together the eggs.</span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: small;">Add in the cheese, salt, garlic powder, pepper and veggies.</span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: small;">Separate the egg mixture into your muffin cups so that they're 2/3 full (make sure to grease the pan if you're not using silicon or paper cups).</span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: small;">Bake for 20-25 minutes until slightly browned and set. </span></li>
	<li><span style="font-size: small;">Eat right away or store in the fridge for later (they should keep for about 4-5 days).</span></li>
</ol>
