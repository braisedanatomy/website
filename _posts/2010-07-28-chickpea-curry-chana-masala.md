<h5><span style="color: #444444;"><span style="color: #808080;">Because we both love to cook (and to eat), aren't huge fans of eating out, and have limited time, Ari and I are going to try to cook several meals on Sunday that we can eat throughout the school week.  Fortunately, Indian food happens to keep well in the fridge and, as the spices meld, gets even better on day 2 or 3.  I'm going to start with an easy recipe, and hopefully entice you to cook along with me as I tackle some of my family's staple dishes.</span></span></h5>
<span style="color: #444444;"><span style="color: #808080;"><img title="Chole2" src="/wp-content/uploads/2010/07/Chole2-1024x682.jpg" alt="" width="640" height="426" /></span></span>
<h5><span style="color: #808080;">Chickpeas are inexpensive and filling, and they play a creamy role in this simple curry.  Think of it as a spicy vegetarian chili.  I'll post later about the importance of the spices, but if you don't have all the spices listed below don't worry--the most important ones are the chili powder and cumin, followed by </span><a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coriander"><span style="color: #808080;">coriander</span></a><span style="color: #808080;"> if you have it.</span></h5>
<span style="line-height: 24px; font-size: 16px;"><span style="color: #808080;">Ingredients</span></span><span style="color: #808080;">
</span>
<ul>
	<li><span style="color: #808080;">1 large yellow onion, diced finely</span></li>
	<li><span style="color: #808080;">4 tbsp neutral oil (i.e. canola)</span></li>
	<li><span style="color: #808080;">3 cans garbanzo beans (16 oz each)</span></li>
	<li><span style="color: #808080;">1 1/2 tsp chili powder (this is the only truly hot spice, adjust to taste)</span></li>
	<li><span style="color: #808080;">2 tsp cumin powder</span></li>
	<li><span style="color: #808080;">2 tsp coriander powder</span></li>
	<li><span style="color: #808080;">14 oz can diced tomato</span></li>
	<li><span style="color: #808080;">2 tsp amchur powder</span></li>
	<li><span style="color: #808080;">1/8 tsp garam masala</span></li>
	<li><span style="color: #808080;">2 tsp salt</span></li>
</ul>
<h5 style="padding-left: 30px;"><span style="color: #444444;"><span style="color: #808080;">In a stockpot or deep pan, warm the oil on the stove at medium-high heat.  Add a small piece of onion and wait until it sizzles before adding the rest.  Saute the onion until it becomes translucent and soft, about 5 minutes.  Add the garbanzo beans, chili powder, cumin powder, and coriander powder.  Stir to coat for about 3 minutes.  Add the tomato and stir to break up the chunks.  Add the amchur powder, garam masala, and salt to taste, and cook for 5 additional minutes, stirring occasionally.  Then add 3 cups of tap water and stir well.  Let cook on a medium-high flame, stirring occasionally, until the water turns into a thin, spicy gravy among the chickpeas.  Adjust for chili powder and salt. </span></span></h5>
<h5 style="padding-left: 30px;"><span style="color: #444444;"><span style="color: #808080;">Garnish with cilantro as desired.  Serve with rice and pita bread.  For the spice intolerant, I recommend a side of plain yogurt to cut the heat.</span></span></h5>
